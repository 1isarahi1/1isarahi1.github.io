📱 SOEN 357 Mini Project: Health Companion Super App

By: Sarah Malik

This UX/UI case study documents my design process for the Health Companion Super App , a mobile health assistant for tracking medications and appointments.

The study covers: User Research, Persona Creation, User Journey Mapping, Wireframing, Prototyping, and Usability Testing .

📄 View Final Report

🔍 Phase 1: User Research & Personas

📌 Goal: Understand target users & pain points.

🎯 Target Users

  • Individuals with chronic illnesses (e.g., diabetes, hypertension).
  • Elderly users who need simplified digital solutions.
  • Busy professionals struggling with health management.
  • Caregivers managing family members' medications.

👤 User Personas

John Doe (58, Type 2 Diabetes) – Needs simple reminders and doctor appointment scheduling.

Jemima Joe (45, Busy Professional with Hypertension) – Prefers smartwatch integration and non-intrusive notifications.

Sam Smith (65, COPD Patient) – Requires voice assistance, large-text UI, and an SOS button.

🗺️ Phase 2: User Journey Mapping

📌 Goal: Identify user frustrations & opportunities for UX improvement.

👀 Key Issues Identified

  • Confusing onboarding process for elderly users.
  • Excessive notifications disrupting workflows.
  • Emergency button not easily accessible.

📈 Improvements

  • ✔️ Guided onboarding tutorial added.
  • ✔️ Adjustable notification frequency settings.
  • ✔️ Emergency SOS button relocated to home screen.

📖 User Journey Stories

These scripts illustrate how different users interact with the Health Companion Super App.

👨‍🦳 John Doe (58, Type 2 Diabetes Patient)

📌 Scenario: John struggles with medication reminders and blood sugar tracking. His doctor recommends the app.

🔍 Awareness (Curious, Skeptical)

Doctor: "John, I think you’d benefit from a health app that tracks your medication and sugar levels. This one is simple to use."

John takes the brochure skeptically. "Another app? I don’t need something complicated." But when he gets home, he decides to check it out.

📲 Onboarding (Overwhelmed, Cautious)

John downloads the app. "Okay, let’s set this up… What’s all this information? My birthdate, medications, dosage… Wow, this is a lot."

He struggles but completes the setup. The app gives him a tutorial on reminders and syncing with his smartwatch.

⏳ Daily Use (Motivated, Engaged)

The next morning, John gets a reminder: "John, it's time for your morning insulin dose." He marks it as taken and feels relieved.

📊 Tracking (Reassured, Sometimes Confused)

John reviews his progress: "Hmm, my levels look stable, but what does this spike mean?"

He clicks "Ask a Doctor" to schedule a chat with a health consultant.

🔔 Notifications (Reliant, Occasionally Annoyed)

"John, don't forget to take your evening insulin!"

Annoyed, he mutters, "I know, I know... Can I adjust these notifications?" He tweaks the settings.

💡 Pain Points & UX Fixes:
  • Overwhelming onboarding → Add a simpler step-by-step setup
  • Confusing data visualization → Include explanations for trends
  • Too many reminders → Allow user-customized notification settings

👩‍💼 Jemima Joe (45, Busy Professional with Hypertension)

📌 Scenario: Jemima balances work, kids, and health. She needs an app that syncs with her smartwatch.

🔍 Awareness (Interested, Busy)

Jemima reads an article: "Top 5 Apps for Managing Hypertension." She clicks, intrigued.

📲 Onboarding (Rushed, Slightly Overwhelmed)

She downloads the app and syncs it to her smartwatch. "Syncing with Apple Health… Done!" "Finally, something easy!"

⏳ Daily Use (Empowered, In Control)

During a morning workout, the app auto-logs her blood pressure. At 3 PM, she gets a reminder: "Take a 5-minute break to breathe." She ignores it.

📊 Tracking (Motivated, Needs Quick Insights)

Before bed, she checks her weekly progress. "Love that I don’t have to log anything manually. But can this be shorter?"

She switches to "Quick View Mode."

🔔 Notifications (Sometimes Dismisses, Needs Customization)

A stress management tip pops up during a meeting. "Practice deep breathing to lower blood pressure."

She customizes reminders to work hours only.

💡 Pain Points & UX Fixes:
  • Overloaded setup → Add a Quick-Start Mode
  • Needs shorter summaries → Introduce a Quick View Dashboard
  • Inconvenient reminders → Allow work-hour settings

🧓 Sam Smith (70, Elderly User with COPD)

📌 Scenario: Sam, a retired carpenter, has limited tech experience but needs simple health tracking.

🔍 Awareness (Skeptical, Needs Guidance)

His daughter installs the app. “Dad, this will remind you about your meds.”

📲 Onboarding (Confused, Frustrated)

Sam squints: "Where’s the button to make this bigger?" His daughter enables "Senior Mode" with larger text and voice instructions.

⏳ Daily Use (Comfortable, Relieved)

A voice notification reminds him: "Sam, it’s time to take your medication." He taps "Taken." "Nice! That was easy."

📊 Tracking (Appreciative, Needs Support)

Sam checks his oxygen levels but doesn’t understand the graph. He taps “Explain”, and a voice assistant provides a breakdown.

🔔 Notifications (Essential, but Not Overwhelming)

At night, he gets a bedtime reminder: "Don’t forget to charge your oxygen monitor."

💡 Pain Points & UX Fixes:
  • Small text & complicated UI → Enable Senior Mode
  • Confusing graphs → Add voice explanations
  • Needs gentle reminders → Use voice-based alerts

🧑‍💻 Phase 4: Usability Testing

📌 Goal: Improve UI based on real user feedback.

🔍 Key Findings

  • 🚫 Complex onboarding – Added a step-by-step guide.
  • 👀 Tiny text – Increased font size and contrast.
  • 🔔 Too many notifications – Users can now adjust frequency.
  • ⚠️ Hidden emergency button – Relocated for quick access.

📊 Hypothetical Usability Testing Plan

This usability test aims to evaluate the Health Companion Super App’s user experience by identifying potential pain points and areas for improvement.

1️⃣ Overview

Goal: Assess how well users navigate onboarding, medication reminders, health tracking, and accessibility features.

2️⃣ Testing Objectives

The test aims to answer key usability questions:

  • Can new users easily complete onboarding?
  • Is the app’s navigation intuitive for different user types?
  • How efficiently can users set up and manage medication reminders?
  • Are users able to understand and utilize health tracking features?
  • Do users find notification customization flexible and effective?
  • How accessible is the app for elderly and less tech-savvy users?

3️⃣ Hypothetical Test Participants

To simulate real-world scenarios, the test includes diverse hypothetical users:

  • 👨‍🦳 Middle-aged diabetes patient needing medication tracking.
  • 👩‍💼 Busy professional with hypertension relying on smartwatch integration.
  • 🧓 Elderly user struggling with small text and complex navigation.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧 Caregiver managing a family member’s health.
  • 📲 Tech-savvy individual using wearables and tracking apps.

4️⃣ Test Scenarios & Tasks

Task No. Scenario User Goal Metrics
1️⃣ User completes the onboarding process. Successful account creation. Time taken, errors, confusion levels.
2️⃣ User sets up a daily medication reminder. Ensures medication adherence. Steps taken, success rate, completion time.
3️⃣ User logs a health metric (e.g., blood pressure). Tracks health data efficiently. Input accuracy, ease of access.
4️⃣ User customizes notification settings. Personalizes alerts effectively. Success rate, ease of finding settings.
5️⃣ Elderly user locates and uses SOS feature. Ensures quick emergency access. Completion rate, response time.
6️⃣ User reviews weekly health trends. Gains insights into health progress. Understanding level, frustration points.

5️⃣ Hypothetical Testing Methodology

📌 Testing Format
  • 🎤 Moderated Testing – Observers guide users remotely or in-person.
  • 📱 Unmoderated Testing – Users complete tasks independently using screen recording tools.
  • ⚖️ A/B Testing – Comparing different UI versions (e.g., default vs. accessibility mode).
📊 Data Collection Methods
  • 📌 Observation Logs – Tracking hesitation points.
  • 📊 Survey Ratings – Users rate tasks (1-5 scale).
  • 📝 Follow-Up Interviews – Gathering improvement suggestions.

6️⃣ Hypothetical Data Analysis & UX Fixes

Issue Impact Level Proposed Fix
Complex onboarding process High Step-by-step tutorial mode.
Difficulty finding SOS button High Move to a prominent location.
Text too small for elderly users Medium Enable "Senior Mode."
Confusing health log navigation Medium Add tooltips & clearer labels.

7️⃣ Future Iterations

After implementing changes, another usability test may be conducted to validate improvements. This ensures the app evolves into a more user-friendly and accessible tool.
